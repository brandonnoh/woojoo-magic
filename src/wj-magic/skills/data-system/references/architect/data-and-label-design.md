# Data & Label Design Reference

## Data inventory checklist

For each candidate input:

- owner/source
- API/download/manual access
- license/commercial constraints
- spatial or entity granularity
- temporal granularity
- update cadence
- publication latency
- historical depth
- missingness
- revisions/backfills
- training availability
- inference-time availability
- cost

## Label taxonomy

### Ground truth
Direct measurement of the outcome.

### Proxy label
Observable variable correlated with the desired outcome.

### Weak label
Approximate label generated by rules, distant supervision, heuristics, or noisy processes.

### Human label
Annotated by reviewers; requires inter-rater agreement and adjudication rules.

### Synthetic label
Simulation/model generated. Use carefully and keep provenance.

## Common traps

### Positive-unlabeled
Known positives exist, but unobserved cases are not necessarily negatives.

### Survivorship bias
Only surviving/active/successful entities appear in the data.

### Selection bias
Data exists only for places/users/items that were selected for measurement or intervention.

### Label delay
Outcome becomes known long after prediction.

### MNAR missingness
Missing values are related to the outcome or process itself.

## Geospatial notes

For spatial problems:
- do not treat adjacent samples as independent
- track CRS and raster resolution
- distinguish point, polygon, raster, grid
- document interpolation assumptions
- avoid assigning “no record” as “no phenomenon”
